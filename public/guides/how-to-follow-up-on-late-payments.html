How to follow up on late payments, politely

Chasing money feels awkward, so it is easy to put off. A short, fixed routine makes it routine instead of personal. Here is one you can start today.

The follow-up schedule

WhenWhat to send
When you deliverThe invoice, with the due date and how to pay.
1 day after the due dateA friendly nudge. It may simply have slipped through.
7 days afterA clear, firmer message that asks for a date.
14 days afterA last polite reminder, and a phone call if you have their number.
After thatStop sending messages. Talk, or pause new work until the old invoice is settled.

Sample messages

Change the names and numbers. Keep them short.

Day 1: friendly

Hi Asha, a quick reminder that invoice INV-1042 for ₹25,000 was due yesterday. It may just have slipped through. You can pay here: [link]. If you have already paid, thank you, and please send me the UTR so I can match it.

Day 7: firm

Hello Asha, following up on invoice INV-1042 for ₹25,000, which is 7 days overdue. Could you please clear it this week, or tell me the date I can expect the payment? If something is holding it up, let me know and we can sort it out together.

Day 14: last reminder, still polite

Hello Asha, this is my last reminder about invoice INV-1042 for ₹25,000, which is 14 days overdue. I would like to close this properly, so please let me know today when the payment will be made, or if there is any problem with the invoice or the work.

What makes a reminder work

  • Make the next step obvious. Include the pay link or the UPI ID in the message. Every extra step you ask of the client is a reason to delay.
  • Name the invoice, the amount and the date. Vague messages get vague answers.
  • Ask for a date, not a promise. "When can I expect this?" gets a concrete answer.
  • Offer the way out. "If there is a problem with the invoice, tell me" turns silence into a conversation.
  • Pick the channel the client reads. If they answer WhatsApp faster than email, use WhatsApp.

What not to do

  • Do not threaten legal action, publicly shame the client, or contact people other than the client. It rarely helps, can backfire, and can create problems for you.
  • Do not send many messages in a day. Once a week is plenty after the first nudge.
  • Do not keep sending after someone asks you to stop. Respect an opt-out.
  • Do not do more work on credit for a client with an old unpaid invoice.

If the client says they paid

Ask for the UTR and check your bank statement. A payment can take a day to show, can be sent to the wrong account, or can be sent as invoice minus TDS (see our guide on TDS for freelancers). On Paivo, the client can enter the UTR on the pay page and you confirm it after seeing the credit.

If the client goes quiet

  1. Call once. A voice is harder to ignore than a message.
  2. Write a short, calm email summarising the invoice, the dates of your reminders and what you need.
  3. If the amount is large, speak to a CA or a lawyer about the options in your case. We do not give legal advice.

For the future, ask for an advance and deliver final files only after payment (see how to get paid before sending final files).

A short phone script

If you call (day 14 or later), keep it to a minute: "Hi Asha, it's Ravi. I'm calling about invoice INV-1042 for ₹25,000, which was due two weeks ago. Is there anything stopping it, so I can help fix it? What date can I expect it?" Then write down the date they give you and send it as a message right after the call: "Thanks for the call. As discussed, I'll expect the payment on 5 December."

Keep a log

For every overdue invoice, note the date of each reminder, the channel, and what the client said. It takes a minute and it turns "I think I asked a couple of times" into a clear record if you ever need to show it to your CA or a lawyer. When to pause new work

It is reasonable to tell a client that you will start the next project once the earlier invoice is settled. Say it as a plain rule, without anger: "I'm happy to start the next one as soon as invoice INV-1042 is cleared." A client with a genuine mix-up will often fix it quickly when new work depends on it.

Reminders and the client's privacy

Only send reminders to the person or team who owes you, using the contact details they gave you for billing. Offer a way to stop: if a client asks you to stop emailing, stop and switch to a phone call or a direct message. Paivo's email reminders carry an unsubscribe link for the same reason.

This is general information, not tax or legal advice. Check with a CA before you act on it.
