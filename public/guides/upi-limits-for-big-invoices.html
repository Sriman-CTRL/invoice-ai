UPI limits for big invoices

UPI is the fastest way for a client to pay you, until the invoice is big. Then a payment can fail halfway through the client's day. Here is how the limits work in general, and what to do about it.

Why UPI payments fail on large amounts

There is no single UPI limit. Several different caps can apply to one payment, and the lowest one wins:

  • NPCI's limits for the type of payment. Paying a person (P2P) and paying a merchant (P2M) are treated differently, and some categories of merchants have higher limits than others.
  • The client's bank can set its own, lower, per-transaction and per-day limits on its customers.
  • The receiving account or app can have its own cap, depending on how your UPI ID is set up (personal or business) and your bank.
  • A daily total across all the client's UPI payments, so an earlier payment that day can use up the room.

NPCI has changed its limits more than once in recent years, and many guides online are out of date.

We have not printed the UPI limit amounts here. On 24 Nov 2026 we could not open the official page that states it, and a number from a blog is not good enough for something that affects your tax.

Where to check: NPCI's UPI pages and circulars at npci.org.in, then your own bank's help page for the limits on your account. Your client should check theirs.

What to do for a big invoice

  1. Offer bank transfer next to UPI. NEFT, IMPS and RTGS are meant for larger amounts. Put your account name, number and IFSC on the invoice, so the client can choose. Paivo's pay page shows UPI and bank details side by side, and suggests bank transfer for large amounts.
  2. Split the invoice. Ask for an advance and a balance, or milestones. Smaller payments clear UPI limits and reduce your risk.
  3. Let the client pay in parts, if they prefer. Two UPI payments on two days work. Paivo accepts partial payments and keeps the invoice "partly paid" until the balance arrives.
  4. Ask the client to try early in the day and to tell you if the bank blocks it. A failed payment is common, and usually fixed by raising a limit in the client's banking app or paying by transfer.
  5. Use a business account for business invoices. If you invoice often, a current account or a business UPI ID may give you better receiving options than a personal one. Ask your bank.

Always ask for the UTR

Whatever route the client uses, ask for the reference number (the UTR) after paying. It lets you find the credit in your bank statement quickly, and it protects both sides if there is a dispute. On Paivo, the client enters the UTR on the pay page; you check your bank and confirm in one click.

A note on the pay-by-link route

Payment gateways take the money first and pay you after deducting a fee. Direct UPI and bank transfer have no gateway fee, but the confirmation is manual. For a big invoice, that trade is usually worth it: you see the credit in your own account and you confirm it yourself.

How to find out your own limit

  1. Open your bank's UPI or payments page, or your UPI app's settings, and look for "transaction limit" or "daily limit". Many banks list it per account.
  2. Ask the client to do the same for their account. Their limit is the one that stops a payment to you.
  3. If either side has a business or current account, ask the bank whether its limits are different from a savings account.
  4. Do a small test payment (₹1 to ₹10) the first time, so a UPI ID typo does not surprise you on the big one.

An example plan for a large project (made-up numbers)

Say the fee is ₹4,00,000. You do not need to know any limit to design an invoice that avoids the problem:

  • 30% advance by UPI before you start, which is a modest amount that almost any account can send.
  • 40% after the first delivery, by UPI or bank transfer, the client's choice.
  • 30% on the final delivery, by bank transfer. You release the final files after you see it in your account.

Each step is smaller, each has its own invoice and its own reference number, and none of them depends on a single large UPI payment going through.

What failed payments look like

  • "Limit exceeded" or "daily limit reached": the client's bank or app limit. Try again after midnight, raise the limit in the app, or use bank transfer.
  • "Payment pending" or "processing": the money may have left the client's account and may reach you later or be returned. Do not ask the client to pay a second time until you have checked your statement and they have checked theirs.
  • "Declined by receiver" or an unknown-ID error: usually a wrong UPI ID. Check it character by character against your invoice.

When a payment fails, the client should keep the screenshot and the transaction reference, even for a failed attempt. It helps if money has been debited and not credited.
