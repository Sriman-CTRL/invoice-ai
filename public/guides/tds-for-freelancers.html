TDS for freelancers: the plain-words version

TDS is tax your client deducts before paying you and deposits with the government in your name. It is not an extra cost: it is advance income tax that you claim credit for later. The confusing part is what reaches your bank, and how to prove it.

What TDS means for you

When a business client pays you, the law may require them to keep a small percentage back and send it to the tax department, quoting your PAN. You receive the rest. At filing time, the deducted amount counts toward your income tax. If you paid more TDS over the year than your final tax, you get a refund.

Example, with a made-up rate to show the arithmetic: invoice of ₹50,000 plus ₹9,000 GST. If the client deducts 10% on the ₹50,000, the TDS is ₹5,000 and ₹54,000 reaches your bank. Try your own numbers in our free TDS calculator (you type the rate; see below for why).

194C and 194J: which is which

These are section numbers under the Income-tax Act as it stood for many years. In plain words:

  • 194C is the section for payments to contractors: someone doing a piece of work for a client, such as production or printing work.
  • 194J is the section for fees for professional or technical services: for example consulting, legal, design or software work billed as a professional service.

Which one your client uses depends on the nature of your work and how their accountant classifies it, and the rates and the yearly limits under which no TDS applies are different for each. Two freelancers doing similar work can be treated differently by different clients.

We have not printed TDS rates, limits or the current section numbers for this financial year here. On 24 Nov 2026 we could not open the official page that states it, and a number from a blog is not good enough for something that affects your tax.

Where to check: the Income Tax Department at incometax.gov.in, which publishes the TDS rate chart for each year. Section numbers and rates can change from one year to the next, so always read the chart for the year of the payment.

Things that change what you receive

  • PAN: give your client your PAN. Without it, the tax law can require a higher deduction. Ask your CA for the current rule.
  • GST on the invoice: clients differ on whether TDS is worked out on the amount before GST or on the total. If GST is shown separately, many clients deduct only on the pre-GST amount, but your client's accountant decides. Agree this before you invoice.
  • Small payments: there are limits below which no TDS is deducted, which is why a client may deduct from one invoice and not another. Do not assume; look at the payment advice.

How to show TDS on your records

  1. Invoice the full amount. TDS is not a discount; do not reduce the invoice.
  2. When the money arrives, note two numbers: what reached your bank and what was deducted.
  3. Ask your client for the TDS certificate they are supposed to issue, and keep it.
  4. After the client files their TDS return, the deduction should show up under your PAN in Form 26AS and in your AIS (Annual Information Statement) on the income tax portal. Check these every quarter, not only in July.
  5. If a deduction is missing, tell the client early. They have to correct their return, and that takes time.

Why does an invoice stay "unpaid" after the client paid?

Because the client sent invoice minus TDS. The invoice is for the full amount, and the gap is the tax deposited in your name. In Paivo you can record what you received; the TDS helper on an invoice shows what to expect before the client pays, so you are not surprised by a shortfall.

Questions to ask a new client before the first invoice

  1. Will you deduct TDS on my invoices? Under which section do you treat this work?
  2. Is it calculated before or after GST?
  3. Which PAN and name should the deduction be filed under? (It should match yours exactly. A mismatch is a common reason a deduction does not show up.)
  4. When will you file your TDS return and issue the certificate?

When the numbers do not match

If your Form 26AS shows less than the client says they deducted, the usual causes are a wrong PAN, a deduction not yet filed, or a deduction booked under a different period. Send the client the amount, the date of payment and your PAN, and ask them to check. Keep every email: if it is still wrong at filing time, your CA can use the paper trail.

This is general information, not tax or legal advice. Check with a CA before you act on it.
