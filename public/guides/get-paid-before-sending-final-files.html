Get paid before you send the final files

The most expensive late payment is the one where the client already has your work. These habits keep the final files with you until the money is in.

Three habits that protect you

1. Ask for an advance

For a new client, many freelancers ask for part of the fee up front (half is common) before starting. It filters out people who were never going to pay and it covers your time if the project stops. Put it on a proper invoice, so there is a record.

2. Split big work into milestones

Instead of one invoice at the end, agree stages: for example the first draft, the revision round, and the final delivery. Invoice each one when it is reached. The client pays in smaller steps, and the most you can lose is one stage.

3. Keep the final files until the last payment arrives

Share previews freely: low-resolution images, watermarked designs, a link to a demo. Send the source files, the full-resolution work or the repository access only after the last payment is in your account. Tell the client this on day one, in the proposal, so nobody is surprised.

Say it politely, up front

I share drafts as we go. The final files are released when the final invoice is paid. This is how I work with every client.

"With every client" matters. It makes the rule about your process, not about whether you trust this person.

How Paivo handles the locked link

This is what the product does, and only this:

  1. When you create an invoice, you can paste a link to the final files (Google Drive, Figma, a repository, a download link). Paivo stores the link, not the files.
  2. Your client opens the pay page. It says that final files are attached, but the link itself is not sent to their browser yet.
  3. The client pays you directly by UPI or bank transfer. Paivo never holds the money.
  4. The client enters the UTR (the transaction reference) on the pay page.
  5. You check your bank. If the money is there, you confirm in one click in your Action Queue.
  6. When the invoice is fully paid, the link appears on the pay page. If the client paid only part, the invoice stays "partly paid" and the link stays hidden.

Entering a UTR does not unlock anything by itself. Only your confirmation does, so a made-up UTR cannot open your files.

What this does not do

  • It is not escrow. Paivo does not hold files or money. It controls when a link is shown.
  • The link itself is only as private as you make it. Once someone has the link, they can share it. Set the sharing options on the file host (Drive, Figma and so on) to match what you want, and use an expiring link if the host supports it.
  • Confirmation is manual. You need to see the payment in your bank before you confirm it in Paivo.
  • It cannot make a client pay. It only makes sure you do not hand over the work first.

A simple checklist

  1. Write your payment terms in the proposal: advance, milestones, release on final payment.
  2. Create the invoice with a due date and the final files link.
  3. Send the pay link, not the files.
  4. Confirm only after you see the money in your bank.
  5. Thank the client, and send the files.

An example flow for a design project (made-up numbers)

  1. Day 0: you agree ₹30,000 for a brand kit. You send invoice 1 for a 50% advance (₹15,000). The client pays by UPI and you confirm it.
  2. Day 7: you share watermarked previews of the logo. The client asks for changes.
  3. Day 12: the work is approved. You send invoice 2 for the remaining ₹15,000, with the link to the final files attached on the invoice, and share only the pay link.
  4. Day 13: the client pays and enters the UTR. You see the credit in your bank and confirm in one click. The link appears on the pay page, and you send a short thank-you message.

If a client objects

  • "You don't trust me?" Reply that it is how you work with every client, and that it protects them too: they get a clear receipt, and you both know what has been delivered.
  • "Send the files, I'll pay tomorrow." Offer the preview now and the files as soon as the payment shows. If the payment is really coming tomorrow, nothing is lost.
  • "I paid, why can't I see the files?" Ask for the UTR. If you can see the money, confirm it. If you cannot, ask them to check the transaction status; UPI payments can be delayed or reversed.
  • "Can I pay half and get half the files?" Only if you have designed deliverables in parts. Otherwise split the work into milestones with their own invoices.

What to put in your proposal

One paragraph is enough: the fee, the advance, the milestones, the due dates, the fact that final files are released after the final payment, and that revisions beyond an agreed number are billed separately. When it is written down and the client says yes in an email, you have both a reference and a calmer conversation later.
