# Finding and talking to your first 10 users

You have a working product. What you don't have yet is proof that someone wants it.
The next 2 weeks are about **conversations, not code**.

The goal is NOT to sell. It is to learn, in 15 minutes per person:
1. How do they chase payments today?
2. How much does it hurt (time, stress, money lost)?
3. Would the "files unlock after payment" idea change what they do?

If 5 of 10 people say "yes, I'd use that this month", build more. If they shrug, change the idea, not the code.

---

## 1. Who exactly to talk to

Pick **one** type first. Suggested: **freelance designers, developers, video editors and writers in India who invoice 3+ clients a month**, especially those who deliver digital files (Figma, Drive, GitHub links).
They feel the "client vanished after delivery" pain most and can use the file-lock feature.

Don't start with big agencies or accountants. Too slow, too many decision-makers.

## 2. Where to find them (start warm, then cold)

Early-stage playbooks agree on the order: **people you know, then people they introduce, then people you find one at a time**
([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-win-your-first-10-b2b-customers),
[Mistral VC playbook](https://mistral.vc/playbooks/first-ten-customers)).

**Warm (do these first, this week)**
- Message 20 people you know: classmates, ex-colleagues, relatives, friends who freelance or run a small business.
- Ask each: *"Who is the one person you know who complains most about clients not paying?"* and request an intro.
- Post once on your own WhatsApp status / LinkedIn: "I'm building a tool for freelancers who get paid late. Looking for 10 people to talk to for 15 minutes. Coffee/chai on me (UPI)."

**Communities (free, reachable now)**
These came up in research; check each one's rules and activity first, as member counts come from third-party listings:
- Reddit **r/FreelanceIndia** (about 57k members) and **r/sidehustleindia** (about 78k)
- **Freelance India Club**: Discord, WhatsApp group and LinkedIn page ([freelanceindia.club](https://www.freelanceindia.club/))
- **Freelance Hub** Discord server for Indian freelancers
- Fiverr India community events for sellers

**How to post in communities without being spammy**
- Read the rules. Many ban promotion.
- Do NOT post your link. Post a *question*: "Freelancers: how do you handle clients who go quiet after you deliver? Genuinely curious, doing research." Then reply to everyone helpfully and DM only people who say it happens to them.
- Contribute for a few days first (answer questions) so you aren't a stranger.

**LinkedIn / Instagram / X (cold, personal)**
Search "freelance designer", "freelance developer", "video editor" + a city. Open 30 profiles. Send the message below to people whose recent posts show they're actively freelancing.
Benchmarks suggest cold outreach needs roughly 30-70 names per customer, so build a list of 50+ before you start.

## 3. The messages (copy, edit, send)

**Cold DM (keep it short, reference one specific fact about them)**
> Hi {name}, I saw your {portfolio/post about X}. Nice work on {specific thing}.
> I'm researching how freelancers deal with late or missing payments. I'm not selling anything. Could I ask you 3 quick questions (15 min, or even by voice note)? Happy to send ₹200 via UPI as thanks.

**Warm intro request**
> Do you know anyone who freelances and has had a client delay or skip payment? I'm talking to a few people for a project and would really appreciate an intro. Takes 15 minutes.

**If they say yes: schedule or ask by voice note.** Voice notes get replies far more often than "let's schedule a call".

**Follow-up (once, after 3 days)**
> No worries if you're busy. Even a one-line answer helps: what's the most annoying part of getting paid by clients?

## 4. The 15-minute conversation (ask, don't pitch)

Ask about the **past**, not the future. Past behaviour is true; opinions about hypothetical tools aren't.

1. Tell me about the last time a client paid late or didn't pay. What happened?
2. What did you do? How long did it take? How did you feel?
3. How do you invoice and collect today? (tools, WhatsApp, UPI, bank)
4. How do you know when a payment has actually arrived?
5. Has a client ever taken your work and stopped replying? What did you do after?
6. What have you tried to fix this? Why did it not work?
7. *(Only now, 2 minutes)* "I'm building a payment page where the final files unlock once the client pays. Would you have used that on your last project? What would worry you?"
8. Who else do you know who has this problem? Could you introduce me?

Rules: let them talk 80% of the time. Don't defend the product. Write down their exact words.

## 5. Keep a simple log

A spreadsheet with one row per person:

| Name | Where found | Date | Last bad payment story | Tools used | Would use file-lock? (Y/N/maybe) | Quote | Next step |

After 10 rows, look for patterns:
- Same complaint in 5+ rows means it's real.
- If "files unlock" gets mostly "maybe/no", the pain is elsewhere (late payment chasing? advances? scope?). Follow where the pain actually is.

## 6. Turning conversations into users (only after they confirm the pain)

- Offer to **set it up for them**. Do their first invoice live over a call. Watch where they get confused.
- Ask them to use it for **one real invoice** this week and tell you what broke.
- Ask: "If this saved you from one late payment, what would that be worth to you?" This is how you discover a price, not by guessing.

## 7. What to build next depends on what you hear

Likely patterns, and what each means:

| You hear | It means | Build |
|---|---|---|
| "Chasing is awkward and takes my time" | Reminders are the product | Real sending (domain + email service) and reply-reading |
| "I never know if money actually arrived" | Confirmation is the pain | Auto-confirm (bank alert emails, or a gateway) |
| "Clients take files and vanish" | File-lock is the product | Make the lock the whole story: milestone / advance payment pages |
| "I use Zoho and it's fine" | You're not different enough | Pick a narrower niche or change the idea |

## 8. A 14-day plan

- **Days 1-2:** build a list of 50 names (20 warm + 30 cold). Set up the log spreadsheet.
- **Days 3-5:** send 20 warm messages and post 2 questions in communities. Book 3 conversations.
- **Days 6-10:** send 10 cold DMs a day. Aim for 10 conversations total.
- **Days 11-12:** write up patterns. Share what you learned with the people who helped.
- **Days 13-14:** decide: double down, narrow, or change direction. Then, and only then, plan the next build.

## 9. It's normal to feel stuck

Feeling stuck after building is very common: building feels productive, talking to strangers feels scary.
The fear is the signal that this is the part that matters. Ten honest conversations will tell you more than another month of code.
